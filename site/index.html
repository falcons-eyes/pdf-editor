Three steps. Your original stays as it is.

Sealworks works on a copy and only writes a new PDF when you ask it to.

  1. Open a PDF

    Click Open new PDF, or drop a file onto the window.

  2. Place your signature

    Click a signature or stamp in the list, or drag it into place. Drag a corner to resize it, the round handle to rotate it.

  3. Save a new file

    Save as file writes a new PDF, like Contract_signed.pdf. Turn on Seal and save to make later edits show.

Seal it, and any later edit shows.

Turn on Seal and save and Sealworks adds a PDF digital signature (PAdES) that covers every byte of the finished file. If anyone changes it afterwards, even by one character, Verify seal shows it.

A seal can’t stop anyone from editing a copy. It makes edits impossible to hide.

The first time you seal, you type your name (and organization, if you like). Sealworks makes a private key and a certificate on your computer, and that name goes into every file you seal.

What Verify seal tells you

  • Seal intactNothing in the file has changed since it was sealed.
  • Modified after sealingThe file changed after sealing, for example re-saved in another app. Ask the sender for it again.
  • Seal is not validThe seal doesn’t match the contents, or it’s damaged. Don’t rely on the file.
  • Not sealedThe PDF has no seal, so there’s no way to tell whether it changed.

Checking a seal, and what it can’t do

A seal is only useful if you know how to read it. Here’s the whole story, limits included.

  • In Sealworks

    Click Verify seal, then drop the PDF on the dialog or choose the file. It’s only checked, never added to your Library. You also see who sealed it, when, and the certificate fingerprint.

  • In Adobe Acrobat

    Open the Signatures panel to see whether the document changed after signing. Acrobat will also say the signer can’t be verified. That’s expected: the certificate was made on the sender’s computer, not bought from a certificate authority.

  • Who sealed it

    The name in a seal is what the sealer typed. To be sure a file came from a particular person, compare fingerprints. The sender finds theirs under Sealer… and tells you once, by phone or in person.

Honest limits

  • It shows tampering; it doesn’t prevent it. Someone can remove the seal and pass the file on as an ordinary PDF. If a seal matters to you, check that one is there.
  • Re-saving in another app usually breaks the seal. macOS Preview can do this even when you only add a note or highlight. Keep sealed files exactly as you received them.
  • It isn’t a certified electronic signature under any law. The seal is for spotting changes. If a document needs a particular kind of electronic signature, check what the recipient or the law requires.
  • Back up your sealer key. It lives only on your computer. Use Sealer… › Back up… to save a password-protected .p12 file. How to back up and restore

Made for the signing line

Everything you need to put a signature on a page, and nothing that sends your files anywhere.

  • Draw your signature

    With a mouse or trackpad, in black, blue or red ink and three weights.

  • Clean stamp photos

    Import or paste a PNG or JPG. For a photo of a stamp on paper, Sealworks removes the white background.

  • Text and date stamps

    Your name, today’s date, “Approved” or “Copy”, in sans, handwriting or serif type.

  • A Library that saves as you go

    Documents and the signatures you placed are kept automatically. Each one shows whether it’s been saved as a file.

  • English or 한국어

    Switch from the status bar or View › Language. Nothing reloads, nothing is lost.

  • Precise placementSize, rotation, opacity and ink color. Type a position in mm or snap to the guides. ⌘Z / Ctrl+Z undoes a mistake.
  • Many pagesCopy an item to other pages, or put it on every page at the same spot.
  • Add once, reuseSignatures and stamps you add stay in the list for the next document.
  • Notices changed filesIf an original or a saved file is moved, renamed or edited in Finder or File Explorer, Sealworks tells you.
